Work With Me

Senior design leadership, and the hands to back it up

I partner with companies that need senior design leadership and the hands to back it up. Sometimes that's a full-time executive role. Sometimes it's a fractional engagement where you get the strategy, the team-building, and the craft without the full-time hire. Either way I sit on the same side of the table as you and your team, focused on what we can actually ship.

An engagement is a season. A workshop is a day. Most people start with the second one.

How much design do you actually need?

Most companies over-invest in design too early or starve it for too long. Before you hire anyone, I'll read where your org actually is and point you to the right level of investment for your stage.

Level 01

AI-assisted

The right tools, no dedicated designers. Enough to ship and learn while design isn't the bottleneck yet.

Level 02

Fractional lead

One senior designer, part-time. Direction and craft without a full-time hire. Often where I come in.

Level 03

Independent contractors

A few specialists for the moment. Flexible hands when the work comes in bursts.

Level 04

External firm

An agency for a big, bounded push. Capacity and polish, on a clock.

Level 05

In-house team

A built-out design org. The right call once design is core to how you compete.

I'll tell you which rung you're actually on, even when the honest answer is "you don't need me yet." I would rather lose the work than sell you the wrong shape of it.

A day that ends in an artefact, not a summary

Most workshops produce a photograph of a wall and a follow-up email nobody opens. These end in something a team can act on: a written rule set, a ranked map, a tested concept. Every session below is one I run myself, and every one names the conditions under which you should not book it.

We bought the tools, we ran the pilot, and it did not turn into a product.

Two days getting the rules out of three long-tenured people's heads and into a table a machine can read. Ends with a policy set and a register of every rule that turned out to live only in a person.

Two days6 to 10 people
Everyone has an AI idea and nobody can say which one is worth doing first.

Walk every function twice, once for the customer-facing experience and once for the internal one. Ends with a ranked map where "we never wrote the rules down" shows up as a visible blocker instead of a surprise three months in.

One day8 to 14 people
Every customer sees the same nine modules whether they bought them or not.

Three hours, and often the right place to start. Modules by plan tier, crossed with role and locale, in one table. Most teams have never seen theirs written out and are surprised by it.

Three hours5 to 9 people
Nobody can describe end to end what actually happens when a customer does this.

One wall, one real process, every function that touches it in the room. Ends with the timeline, the places the room disagreed, and the boundaries drawn where people actually put them rather than where the org chart says.

Half a day8 to 12 people
It works in the demo and falls apart the moment a real user touches it.

One flow, four passes: where the model produces something, what triggers it, where a human decides, and what the interface does when the model is wrong. The fourth is the one teams skip and the one that decides whether a pilot survives.

Half a day5 to 9 people
We have a design system and every new customer still needs a designer.

Tested against one question: can a new tenant be configured without a designer in the room. Ends with a verdict, component library, kit, or rules engine, and the entitlement, locale and role gaps that decide it.

Rooms I have actually run

SPOTIO, June 2025

Two days, on site, then run again for a second team

The brief named two people rather than a technology: a canvasser and an account manager losing their day to repetitive work. Pre-work was mandatory with a deadline five days out. The room worked a four-colour wall with printed row labels reading ACTION and ACTORS, where the app and the AI were written in as actors alongside the humans. Day two the teams acted the workflow out, current state first and the version with AI in it second, while everyone else watched for edge cases.

Four one-page concept briefs landed within four days, each one quoting the sticky note it came from. Every brief is signed "Author(s): Workshop Participants", so the thing that circulated afterwards belonged to the room and not to me.

That is the part clients remember. The first share of each day ran under a hard no-questions rule so nobody senior could kill a half-formed idea early, observers were given three specific things to write down instead of a chair, and the personas we designed against included the person who did not want to be there. Silos came down because everyone had a voice and the voice was written down with a name on it. Nobody had to argue afterwards about whose idea it was.

SNHU, summer

A five-day design sprint, remote, across two calendar weeks

Ten participants, split explicitly into active participants and observers so nobody had to pretend. A four-step pre-work programme, a written facilitation script, and a day-by-day arc from ideation to a tested concept. My name is printed on the board as one of two facilitators, next to a line telling participants to call me with questions.

What it does not show: what shipped afterwards. That record is not mine to publish, so I do not claim it.
