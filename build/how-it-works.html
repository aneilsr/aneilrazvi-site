1 Stage 1 · Steps 0 to 2

Define

Your idea in one sentence, what done looks like, and everything it could do, sorted on one page.

Paper Day · steps 1 and 2Self-paced

You leave with: the whole app on one page

Step 0

Before you start

A short welcome video, so you arrive knowing how the steps run and what to bring.

Step 1

Outcome first

Write what done looks like in one line: someone can do one specific thing, without asking you how. Everything after this gets easier.

In the recipe example: done when someone can pick tonight's dinner from what's in the house in under two minutes.

Step 2

Mind dump

Get everything your app could do out of your head and onto paper. Then sort it, star what matters, and circle the one part you build first.

In the recipe example: pantry, recipes, a shopping list and the household who shares them, sorted on one page.

2 Stage 2 · Step 3

Paper

Every part of your app on paper, one sheet per part, starting where your users start.

Paper DaySelf-paced

You leave with: the product, before spending a dollar

Step 3

Build it in paper

Sketch every part of the app, one sheet per part, starting where your users start. Paper is fast, cheap and easy to throw away, which is the point.

In the recipe example: one sheet each for home, the pantry, a recipe and the shopping list.

3 Stage 3 · Steps 4 and 5

Test

Hand it to people who have never seen it, watch where they get lost, and fix the paper.

Paper DaySelf-paced

You leave with: a tested design, in photos

Step 4

Break it, twice

Two people who have never seen your app each try one task per part while you stay quiet and watch. The paper goes back to the start before every task.

In the recipe example: a tester tries to find something to cook with what's in the pantry.

Step 5

Fix the paper

Change what broke, after the tester leaves, then photograph the new version. Finding this on paper costs minutes, not weeks.

In the recipe example: if a tester can't find the pantry, the pantry moves.

4 Stage 4 · Steps 6 to 9

Build

Turn the paper into a plan an AI can follow, build it one part at a time, and set up how it takes money.

Build cohortSelf-paced

You leave with: a first version of your app, live

Step 6

Write the plan

Make a Project in Claude or ChatGPT and write four short documents in it: vision, rules, backlog and parking lot. This is what the AI builds from.

In the recipe example: the four documents for the recipe app, in one Project.

Step 7

Pick your stack

One page on what your app runs on, written in the same Project, so the AI and you agree on the tools before anything gets built.

Step 8

Build one part at a time

Build one part per loop, and end every loop by checking it against the "done when" line from your backlog. Home and your first part go live at a web address you can share.

In the recipe example: home and the pantry, built, checked and live.

Step 9

Take money (optional)

If your app will charge, set up how it takes money, with the questions to ask an accountant or attorney before you do.

Two questions people ask here

Do I need to know how to code?

No. The course is built for people who don't. You think on paper, test with real people, and then give an AI tool a plan clear enough to build from.

Will my whole app be finished?

Not every part, and that's on purpose. Your whole app will be designed on paper, tested with real people and planned. Then you build a first working version with an AI tool: real software, live at a web address anyone can open on their phone, with the home screen and your first part working. You leave knowing how to build every other part the same way.
