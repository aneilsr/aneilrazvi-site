Proof it works · Cubby

Cubby went through every part of the path.

Cubby is a video player set for each person, for autistic teens and adults. A caregiver picks the videos, and there's no search, no recommendations and no autoplay. I designed and shipped it solo by directing AI tools. It has been on the App Store since August 2026.

See Cubby live Read the full case study

The path, applied

Four parts, and what each one cost.

This is the same order the course teaches. Where a part came late, it cost more, and that's why the order matters.

Part 1

Vision

Done, kept current
  • It started as a kids' app. A parent hub, child profiles and a curated feed of kid-safe videos.
  • Then the market moved. YouTube Kids shipped "Approved Content Only" for free: a parent handpicks every video and search is off. That was the pitch, released by the incumbent at no cost.
  • Every call went into a decision log, with the question, the evidence and what it cost. Today every AI session on Cubby starts by reading the same few files: the backlog, the decision log, the changelog and the design system.

What it changed: who Cubby is for, before the real build. The decision log is what the free kit calls Decisions.

Part 2

Sketch & test

Done, still testing
  • Caregiver interviews pointed it at teens and adults. The decision rule was written before the calls, so the goalposts couldn't move afterward. The pivot also took Cubby out of the COPPA minefield.
  • Testers moved the center of the product. Two caregivers, separately, asked for control over the picture and the sound. That became the core: brightness, contrast, color, warmth, dim and a volume ceiling, set for each person.
  • Words got tested too. "No escape" was meant as a promise. A tester read it as a threat. It became "No recommendations. No rabbit holes."
"It takes you two hours to select videos and he runs through them in 20 minutes."A dad in the beta. It became bulk add.

What it cost when this came late: a finished design system, thrown away and rebuilt for the new audience. Testing before building is cheaper.

Part 3

Build with AI

Shipping
  • Figma, then a written build plan, then AI tools in small batches, with every change checked on a preview before it ships.
  • A design system with three sensory modes, Calm, Balanced and Engage. The build fails if any color pair drops below WCAG AA.
  • Check it before you trust it. Three bugs once passed a clean build. Every one was caught by opening the live app and reading what was on the screen. Now anything touching sign-in, roles or design tokens gets tested by driving the real product.
Part 4

Launch

Live
  • On the App Store since August 2026, and on the web at cubbyplaylist.com. Free for one person, $4.99 a month for a family.
  • Rules you don't know exist. Store review, privacy rules, and Google's verification for access to YouTube all came up before launch. Cubby keeps no age or date of birth on purpose.
  • Android is in beta. Google Play needs 12 testers opted in for 14 days in a row before an app can go to production.
  • Measure and listen. Since launch: a one-tap sensory-safe preset, bulk add, and a full redesign. The top request now is sharing one account between two adults.
  • Out in public: entered in RevenueCat's Shipaton and demoed at Go Fractional's Demo Day.
